01Problem

Real data can't leave the building

Credit Bureau Singapore is the country's main consumer credit bureau, regulated by the Monetary Authority of Singapore. It collects individuals' credit data (loans, credit cards, repayment history) and helps lenders assess creditworthiness.

Privacy rules mean that real data cannot be used for testing or client demos. CBS was building its demo and test data by hand, which was slow, inconsistent and hard to scale.

The brief: a reproducible system that generates synthetic credit data that behaves like the real thing statistically, for testing new analytics products and producing client demonstration reports.

02Data

Four kinds of credit series

The tool handles four data types: share of wallet, delinquency, new customers and account balance. In the example run shown on our poster, the upload held 15 bank series with 61 data points each; 11 were usable as they were and 4 needed modification.

Before anything was generated, the pipeline filled missing values, standardised formats to a common range and removed duplicates and outliers.

03System

Cluster first, then pick the right generator

Banks don't all behave alike, so the tool groups them before generating anything. The workflow in the Streamlit app:

  1. Upload a CSV of real series.
  2. Cluster banks with similar patterns (K-medoids or K-means), choosing k by silhouette score.
  3. Select synthesis methods to compare.
  4. Set series per cluster, then generate, score and export as CSV or TXT.

Each data type suits different generators, so the tool offers seven, from classical time series to a neural network:

ARIMAAutocorrelation and linear trendAccount balance

MY PARTI led the CTGAN pipeline, the generative adversarial network that creates entirely new rows by training a generator against a discriminator, and designed the team's dual-view dashboard (Customer/Agency) with fidelity scoring, so results could be compared and exported in one place.
04Results

On the account-balance series, Copula scored highest (80.7/100) with the lowest Wasserstein distance (167.1), and CTGAN scored lowest (17.6). That's consistent with our own method guide, which marks CTGAN as suited to large datasets; these series had 61 data points each.

The lesson we built into the tool: the best generator depends on the data type, so instead of betting on one method it scores every candidate and recommends the winner for each series.

05Limits

What we'd check before anyone relied on it

  • Only statistical validation. Fidelity is judged by statistical comparisons, without formal review by CBS analysts.
  • Bounded by the input. Synthetic data stays within the date range of the data provided.
  • General-purpose methods. The generators weren't customised specifically for CBS data.
  • Assumes stable behaviour. It treats the raw data as representative and consumer behaviour as fairly stable over time.

Next steps we proposed: integrate the pipeline into CBS's internal tooling, extend it to more credit metrics and product lines, and revalidate periodically as the real data evolves.
